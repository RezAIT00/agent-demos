# BEAK VELOCITY — build notes

A 72.000 s anime trailer about a pelican riding a bicycle. Picture, lettering, music and sound are all generated by the code in this folder. There are no image, video, audio, font or model files as inputs, no libraries in the renderer, and no generative models.

## Read this first
**I never watched the film play and never heard the soundtrack.** I judged the picture from stills, contact sheets and strips of consecutive frames. I judged the audio from loudness meters, per-shot levels, band-energy measurements and a spectrogram. Timing feel in motion, and whether the guitar, brass and squawk actually sound good, still need a human to watch it with the sound on.

## How it renders
- **Renderer:** my own small WebGL2 engine, with no libraries. The pelican, falcon, bikes, fish and props are meshes built from math every frame (lofted tubes, blades, extrusions). It is real 3D, so the bike stays correct from any angle.
- **Cel look:** three tones with hue-shifted shadows, a hard rim light, a hard shadow-map edge and hard specular bands. A G-buffer holds part ID, depth and a per-vertex line weight. The ink pass reads it at 3840×2160 and resolves to 1080p. Lines taper to nothing at feather tips and are heavier on the underside.
- **Wheels:** the 32 crossed spokes are drawn by a shader that works out rotational blur per pixel, with arc streaks and one reflector. At speed the chain becomes a continuous band with moving dashes.
- **Backgrounds:** painted in shaders with no ink lines. Clouds use two noise textures generated in code at start-up. The ground can be tilted for real downhill shots while the horizon stays level.
- **2D layer:** eye close-ups, speed lines, sparks, smoke, splashes, shatter and lettering on Canvas2D, all computed directly from t.
- **Lettering:** a heavy polygon face, a single-stroke face and four katakana, all defined as coordinates in `src/type.js`.
- **Animation:** characters, wheels, crank and chain update on twos. Camera, ground, sky and most effects run on ones.
- **Sound:** `tools/synth.mjs` (Node, no packages). Karplus-Strong double-tracked guitar with distortion and a cabinet filter, drums, bass, strings, brass, choir, lead, plucks, reverb and delay. About 40 kinds of sound effect, plus wind, chain, freewheel and rain beds driven by what each shot is doing. It masters with its own BS.1770 meter and a look-ahead true-peak limiter. The music is original: an 8-bar theme in E minor that returns in F♯ minor.
- **Sync:** `src/timeline.js` drives both picture and sound. 160 BPM makes one beat exactly 9 frames.
- **GPU load:** about 200 MB of render targets and textures (calculated from their sizes, not measured). A full capture takes about 2 minutes (about 0.06 s per frame).

## Reproduce
```
node tools/build.mjs                                   # src/ -> index.html
node tools/cap.mjs --from 0 --to 72 --fps 24 --preset slow --out tmp/video.mp4
bash tools/finish.sh                                   # soundtrack, mux, X copy, audits
node tools/cap.mjs --png "renderContactSheet()" --out contact_sheet.png
node tools/cap.mjs --png "renderCharacterSheet()" --out character_sheet.png
node tools/cap.mjs --eval "filmRigCheck()"             # drivetrain + leg reach, every frame
node tools/cap.mjs --eval "determinismCheck(96)"
```
Needs Edge, ffmpeg, Node 24, and Python with numpy.

### Brightness and flashes
- **Luma:** 75 frame-to-frame jumps above 12/255. All match a cut or a named event (impact frame, slam, scripted fade, eye opening and so on), with **0 unexplained**. Single-frame pops above 7/255: 6, with 0 unexplained. The list of named events is hand-written in `tools/audit.py`, so "explained" means "falls inside a window I declared intentional".
- **Local one-frame flips** (12×9 block grid): 8 flags outside those windows. I looked at the frames: text slams, the rival's wheel rolling in, the overtake pass and the shock ring after the title slam. I inspected those clusters on earlier renders; the final render reports the same eight frames as the one before the drivetrain fix.
- **Flashes:** worst 1-second window is 1 on the full frame and **2 in the worst quarter-screen region** (limit 3). There are 5 ink-inverted impact frames of 2 frames each (10.50, 27.00, 54.00, 57.38 and 60.00 s), at least 2.6 s apart. This is my own WCAG-style counter, not a certified photosensitivity tool, and it has no separate red-flash test.

## Critique passes and what they changed
**While building (stills, act by act)**
- The first character sheet showed the ball of each foot 6 mm inside the pedal. Fixed, then re-measured.
- The first aura burned the bird and bike into a white blob. Flame now comes only from the bird and paints only over background.
- Faint concentric banding in the spinning wheels. Fixed in the spoke shader.
- The eye close-up's brow looked like a comb, then like a saw blade, and its plumage like roof tiles. Redrawn.
- The first frame stalled for 190 s while D3D compiled the shaders. Noise moved into textures; the stall is now about 5 s.
- The fish punchline was hidden under its own caption, and the shot was too short to read it.

**Pass 1 (contact sheet and stills)**
- Hard cuts to title cards landed on an empty frame.
- The crash was unreadable and the click-in foot barely moved.
- The tagline covered the pelican's head on the title card.

**Pass 2 (frames from the encoded video)**
- The overtake, the climax payoff, passed *behind* the rival. It now blows through the foreground.
- The canyon flight did not read as a canyon. Both ledges are now in frame, with the falcon staring up.
- The rival disappeared into its purple sky. Added a moon behind it.
- The spread wings looked like sticks with ball joints. Added a covered leading edge.
- **The downhill race ran on a flat road.** It now has a real 8.5° slope in the charge, side, spark, taunt and overtake shots.
- The scarf tie did not read. The pelican now holds both ends.

**Found by the checks**
- A one-frame dark pop on "GO!", a stepping heartbeat light, a two-frame final fade and a one-frame stutter in every text slam.
- **Determinism failed: 20 of 72 frames differed.** Fixed by resetting every canvas each frame and rasterising it on the CPU.
- **The crank had come loose from the rear wheel.** Where I capped cadence for legibility, and in several climax shots, the 2.5:1 ratio did not hold, and the cog turned while coasting. I found this by re-reading the code while writing these notes. Wheel angle now comes only from the crank while pedalling, and a whole-film check covers it.

**Is it badass and funny, or generic?** Judging from frames, the strongest material is specific to this bird: the broken-SVG cards, the mid-air scream, pedalling across a canyon with its wings out, and eleven fish delivered with a straight face. The weaker stretches are the generic part: the middle of the training montage and the straight road shots could belong to any cycling trailer.

## Calls I made without asking
- Title **BEAK VELOCITY**, with *A PELICAN RIDING A BICYCLE* as the tagline. Rival: **THE PEREGRINE**.
- "EVERY MACHINE DREW IT WRONG" nods to the SVG meme without naming any model. The bad drawings are my own.
- The flat statement "IT CARRIED THE FISH THE WHOLE RACE." is the punchline card. I added one beat to that shot so it can be read.
- Legs follow bird anatomy (forward knee in feathers, backward hock).
- In fast shots, cadence is capped so legs stay readable on twos. Chain, cog and wheel stay locked to the crank, but the road scrolls faster than the tyre would roll. The spoke blur hides this. It is a deliberate cheat.
- In the hairpin the inside pedal is up and the bike coasts.

## Weak spots
**Three weakest moments**
1. **The crash, 10.50–11.25 s.** The tumbling pelican breaks into a tangle of white and black shapes. It reads more as a burst of feathers than as a bird going over the bars.
2. **Neck and neck, 36.00–38.25 s.** The Peregrine sits half hidden behind the hero, so who is passing whom barely reads. The fish-tail plant here lasts about a third of a second and is small. It pays off for people who rewatch, but many will never see it.
3. **The scarf / GET UP shot, 16.50–18.00 s.** Close to the camera, the wings look like jointed mannequin arms, and the frame cuts both wheels off near the hubs. As a hero beat it is the least "pelican on a bicycle".

**Across the whole film**
- It reads as cel-shaded 3D more than hand-drawn 2D anime. Forms are very smooth, line quality is fairly even, and there are only a few true smear drawings (crash and overtake).
- The folded wing is an arm tube dressed with feather blades. This shows most in medium shots.
- Pines, rocks and road are simple, and the mountains look flat in the bright daylight shots.
- The hairpin, ramp, start and finish are on level ground.
- The flashback reuses earlier shots in sepia.
- On the title card the rider is small, and its trailing aura can look like misregistered print.
- The synthesised instruments have not been auditioned (see top).

## Build time
About 2 h 15 min of wall-clock time on 2026-11-26, from 08:20 to about 10:36 CST, covering treatment, engine, character check, film, score, critique passes and several full re-renders. The GPU and CPU were shared with other sessions.

## One thing to know about the capture tool
The capture script I adapted from an earlier project (read only, not modified) picks a **random** browser debugging port and attaches to whatever answers on it. When several sessions capture at once, a port clash could make one session drive another's headless browser and then close it. My early runs used that scheme. All of them rendered my own page and I saw no sign of a clash, but I cannot rule one out from my side. One later run failed to attach to any browser, which is what made me look. `tools/cap.mjs` here now uses OS-assigned ports and reads the port from its own profile folder. The original file may still be worth the same fix. I did not kill any process by name; the tool only closes the browser it attaches to and the process it spawned.
